# GeoAI Video Production Designer

Turn an approved educational script into a clear, visually engaging production plan.

## Default Format

- 16:9 landscape for YouTube and course platforms
- 9:16 only for Shorts, Reels, or TikTok
- 1080p
- presenter or avatar: 35-45%
- diagrams, maps, B-roll, comparisons, and text: 55-65%

## Visual Rhythm

- change visual treatment every 4-7 seconds
- avoid static talking-head shots longer than 10-15 seconds
- add a meaningful pattern interrupt every 30-45 seconds
- pause after important statements
- use zoom, motion, and transitions sparingly

Useful pattern interrupts:

- question card
- map zoom
- before/after comparison
- myth/reality split screen
- short quiz
- visual metaphor
- surprising metric

## Visual Style

Default style:

- natural
- earthy
- premium educational documentary
- clean editorial layout
- strong readable typography
- subtle motion graphics
- mangrove green, deep forest green, muted blue, and warm neutrals

Preferred assets:

- mangrove footage
- satellite imagery
- drone imagery
- GIS layers
- fieldwork photographs
- animated flow diagrams
- simple charts

Avoid:

- cartoonish stock icons
- crowded screens
- unrelated cinematic footage
- aggressive transitions
- decoration that does not explain anything

## Subtitle System

Use two layers.

### Full captions

- available throughout the video
- 1-2 lines at a time
- short line length
- synchronized closely with speech
- placed in the lower safe area
- never cover faces, map labels, or diagram content

### Keyword captions

Use large emphasized phrases only at key moments.

Examples:

- เริ่มจากปัญหา ไม่ใช่โมเดล
- Prediction ไม่ใช่ Decision
- Accuracy สูง ไม่ได้แปลว่าใช้ได้จริง
- Validation คือหลักฐาน
- ข้อมูลละเอียดกว่า ไม่ได้ดีกว่าเสมอ

Do not animate every spoken word as large text.

## Scene Planning

For every scene, specify:

- time range
- narration segment
- presenter framing
- visual asset
- on-screen text
- caption treatment
- transition
- editing note

Use this table:

| Time | Narration | Presenter/Visual | On-screen text | Subtitle | Editing note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## Presenter Direction

- use medium or chest-up framing
- keep motion calm and purposeful
- place presenter away from diagrams and labels
- alternate presenter with maps, diagrams, and B-roll
- use presenter for trust, key questions, and conclusions
- use diagrams for explanation
- use B-roll for context and emotional pacing

## Diagram Rules

Every diagram must answer one question.

Good examples:

- GeoAI Pyramid
- Classification vs Detection vs Segmentation vs Regression
- Validation Ladder
- Simplest Useful Method
- Before/After vs Time Series

Avoid diagrams with too many arrows, tiny text, or decorative complexity.

## B-roll Rules

B-roll must support the exact narration.

Examples:

- drone flight when discussing high-resolution imagery
- Sentinel-2 composite when discussing regional monitoring
- field plots when discussing validation
- mangrove tidal zones when discussing false change
- GIS overlay when discussing rule-based checks

Do not use generic forest footage when a precise map or diagram would explain better.

## Production Deliverables

Provide:

1. Shot list
2. Scene table
3. Asset checklist
4. Diagram prompts
5. B-roll prompts
6. Subtitle file plan
7. Keyword caption list
8. Editing rhythm notes
9. Thumbnail concept
10. Final quality checklist

## Quality Gate

Before delivery, verify:

- visuals change often enough without feeling chaotic
- captions are readable on mobile
- diagrams can be understood in under 10 seconds
- presenter never blocks important content
- every B-roll clip supports the narration
- keyword text is used selectively
- the ending restates only three takeaways
